## **Step 1: Summary (120–150 words)**

Blockchain is a digital system that records information in a secure and transparent way. Unlike traditional databases, it stores data in “blocks” that are linked together in a chain. Each block contains transaction details, a timestamp, and a unique code called a cryptographic hash, which makes it nearly impossible to alter past records. Since the system is decentralized, no single authority controls it; instead, transactions are verified by multiple participants (nodes). This ensures trust, transparency, and security without the need for middlemen like banks. Blockchain is widely known for supporting cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin, but its applications extend to many industries. It helps track goods in supply chains, ensures the authenticity of digital records, enables smart contracts, and protects sensitive data. Overall, blockchain offers a revolutionary way of storing and sharing information with reliability and security.

## **Step 2: Simplified Version with Examples**

Blockchain is like a digital notebook that is shared by many people. Once something is written in it, no one can erase or change it. The information is stored in “blocks” that are connected to form a “chain,” making it secure and trustworthy. Since it is not controlled by one person or company, everyone can check and confirm the records.

A popular example is **Bitcoin**, where blockchain keeps track of all transactions without needing banks. Another example is **supply chain management**, where companies use blockchain to trace products like medicines or food from the factory to the customer, ensuring quality and safety.

In simple terms, blockchain is a safe, transparent, and reliable way of recording and sharing information.
